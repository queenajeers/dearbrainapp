The STICK Method

5 simple steps to make a good habit stick, even with a phone that wants all your time.

Scroll to start ↓

The problem

It’s not because you’re lazy

You wanted to read your book. You picked up your phone “just for a minute.” Now an hour is gone.

This happens to everyone. And there’s a reason.

Why it happens

Your phone works like a slot machine

Each time you scroll, you might see something fun. Or you might not. Not knowing is exactly what keeps you scrolling.

The STICK Method4

Step 1 · S

Select one small habit

Not five. Just one. Make it so small it feels a bit too easy.

Feeling like doing something goes up and down. A tiny habit still happens on a bad day.

The STICK Method5

Step 1 · S

Easy habits stick faster

Scientists watched people build new habits. Drinking a glass of water became automatic faster than doing 50 sit-ups.

Try this“Read 10 pages,” not “read more.”
The STICK Method6

Step 2 · T

Time it

“I’ll do it later” almost never happens. Later isn’t a time.

Give your habit a set time, and the time itself reminds you.

The STICK Method7

Step 2 · T

91%

In one study, people who wrote down when and where they would exercise actually did it 91% of the time.

Everyone else? About 1 in 3.

Try this“At [time], in [place], I will [habit].”
The STICK Method8

Step 2 · T

Your phone drains your brain

Just having your phone on the table makes it harder to think, even when it’s face down.

During your habit time, put it in another room.

The STICK Method9

Step 3 · I

Itemize it into a tiny list

“Write in my journal” is fuzzy. Your brain doesn’t know where to start.

“Open the notebook. Write the date. Write three lines.” Now it knows exactly what to do.

The STICK Method10

Step 3 · I

Small wins feel big

Ticking a box feels good. Researchers found that making progress, even a tiny bit, is what makes people happiest at work.

Try thisMake your first step super easy, like “put on your shoes.”
The STICK Method11

Step 4 · C

Capture proof

This is the step most people skip.

Our brains are too nice to us. We say “I kind of did it.” Then one day we stop, and we don’t even notice.

The STICK Method12

Step 4 · C

Done is a photo, not a feeling

People reach their goals more often when they track their progress. Even more when they write it down or show someone.

Try thisPick your proof photo today. The open book. The muddy shoes. No photo? Not done.
The STICK Method13

Step 5 · K

Keep a record

Write one line about what you did. Then mark the day.

Workers who wrote for 15 minutes about their day did 23% better on a test.

The STICK Method14

Step 5 · K

The coffee card trick

Buy 10 coffees, get one free. People buy faster as they get close to the free one.

Your calendar works the same way. The more days you mark, the more you want to keep going.

The STICK Method15

Step 5 · K

Never miss twice.

Missing one day doesn’t ruin a habit. Missing two in a row is where it starts to slip.

The STICK Method16

Why winter

The start is the steep part

A new habit takes about 66 days to feel automatic. But the first few weeks count the most.

Start this winter, and by the new year your habit will already be running.
