The BRAIN Method

5 surprising, science-proven tricks to make one habit stick, even when your phone is fighting you.

Scroll to start ↓

The secret

People with the most self-control use it the least

Scientists tracked people through their day. The most self-controlled people didn’t resist harder. They just ran into fewer temptations.

Their habits did the work. So this method doesn’t need willpower.

The BRAIN Method2

Why you scroll

Your brain loves “maybe” more than “yes”

Scientists measured dopamine cells in monkeys. They fired hardest when a reward was a 50/50 chance, not when it was certain.

Every scroll is a maybe. That’s the hook. Now let’s use that wiring for something good.

The BRAIN Method3

B · Become

Say who you are, not what you’ll do

Before a US election, people asked about “being a voter” actually voted more than people asked about “voting.” Researchers checked real voting records.

Try thisNot “I want to read.” Say “I’m a reader.”
The BRAIN Method4

R · Reserve a time

91%

People who wrote one sentence, when and where they’d exercise, did it 91% of the time. Others: about 1 in 3.

And people start more goals on “fresh start” days: a Monday, the 1st of the month, a birthday. November 1 counts.

Try this“At 7 am, in my kitchen, I will read 10 pages.”
The BRAIN Method5

A · Add a treat

Hide something you love inside the habit

In one study, people could hear a gripping audiobook only at the gym. They went 51% more often. Their brain started wanting the gym.

Try thisStart your to-do list with the treat: 1. Play my podcast. 2. Shoes on. 3. Walk.
The BRAIN Method6

I · Inspect

Prove it. Don’t announce it.

In some experiments, people who told others their goal then worked less on it. Saying it felt like doing it.

A proof photo is the opposite: real evidence, just for you.

Try thisNo photo, not done.
The BRAIN Method7

N · Note it

Reward yourself now, not someday

People who got small rewards right away stuck with exercise and study longer than people promised bigger rewards later.

Write one line. Mark the day. That’s your instant reward.

The BRAIN Method8

N · Note it

Never miss twice.

Dieters who slipped once often ate more afterwards: “I’ve already ruined it.” Scientists call this the what-the-hell effect.

One missed day is nothing. Just don’t let it become two.
